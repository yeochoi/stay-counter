Stay Counter design system

Know your days, wherever you are. A calm travel days counter that tells people living between countries how long they can stay, when to leave, and how close they are to a tax limit. It runs as a responsive web app on phones and laptops.

Countdown Ring

The hero of Today. One number in a ring, one labelled date beside it.

  • size-ring ring on hero, with no filled disc. A 6px track in white at 16 percent always shows the whole circle, so every state looks like the same component.
  • During a stay a 6px arc in ring runs inside the track with round ends, showing how much of the stay is left. The number is days left, counted from tomorrow.
  • Before a trip, or on a day between stays, there is no arc, only the track, with days to go.
  • The number is 28 semibold, 34 on laptops, and the unit is small, so the date beside it in display leads.
  • Nothing else in the hero during a stay. No stay count, no next entry chip.

Stay Timeline

The stays of the trip you are on, on Today, and of any trip on its overview. Shows where you are in the trip and what is left to book.

  • A header with the country, like China trip, and nothing else. Each stay already says whether it is booked.
  • One row per stay. Dates in title, a status in small under them, days in small on the right, then a chevron.
  • Status says what each stay is. Done, Now, Back in N days, Booked or Not booked.
  • One line in stay-line joins the stays so they read as one trip. The line into a stay is solid when it is booked, done or now, and dashed while it is not booked.
  • Dots match. done-dot for done, a large primary dot with a current-line halo for now, a filled primary dot for booked, a hollow primary ring for not booked. The now row sits on current.
  • A stay past the end of visa free or a visa gets a small coral warning icon after its dates. The row's screen reader label says the reason. Opening the stay explains it in one sentence.
  • Tapping a stay opens Stay N of M. You can change its dates and turn on I booked travel for this stay. Later stays move along.
  • The card ends with a Trip details row. It shows the total days, or a coral count like 1 to check when the whole trip has a problem that belongs to no single stay.

List Row

A trip in a list. On phones two lines, on laptops one table row.

  • Phones. Country in title, then one small in muted with dates, days in the country and type, like Sep 26 – Oct 2 · 7 days · Personal. Planning is added for a future trip with one stay not yet booked. A trip with several stays says how many are booked, like 1 of 2 booked, or Not booked.
  • Laptops. One row with aligned columns for country, dates, days and type, with no header row. The last column has a fixed width so rows line up across cards.
  • The trip you are in sits in its own card on current with a current-line border and a Now chip with days left.
  • Every row has a chevron and opens the trip overview.
  • Trips is split by tabs, Upcoming and Past. Past groups by year.

Tax Days

Days in each country, counted the way that country counts for tax.

  • One row per country visited, home left out.
  • The second line says the window. In 2026 for a calendar year rule like China, Past 12 months for a rolling rule.
  • Small counts show only the days. From 60 days a bar appears with N of 183 days. Close to the limit the value becomes a coral chip and the bar turns coral.
  • Counts run up to today, so they match what has really happened.

Warning

How the app says something needs checking. Each problem shows once, where it lives.

  • A problem with one stay is a small coral icon after that stay's dates in the timeline. No extra line. The stay screen says the reason in a coral box, like This stay is after visa free entry ends on Dec 31, 2026. You need a visa for it.
  • A problem with the whole trip, like a tax limit or a single entry visa, is a coral count on Trip details, like 1 to check.
  • On the trip overview, one chip under the answer counts the problems, like 2 rules to check. See below. The rules card then puts one small line in coral-text under each rule it breaks.
  • The value in a row keeps its normal style. Only the line under it is coral.
  • Calm words. Rules to check, never broken.
  • A new trip's plan before saving has no rules card, so its warnings sit in one coral box instead.

Chip

Small rounded labels.

  • On hero, chips are white at 10 percent with white small text, like 147 days, Business, Visa free.
  • In content, Now is primary with white text, the close to limit chip is coral-soft with coral-text.
  • Every chip uses regular weight. Its fill already makes it stand out.
  • Chips never stack into a second line. If they would, drop the least useful one.

A quiet grey box that explains why a screen behaves differently.

  • chip background, chip-text in small, radius-note, 12 by 14 padding.
  • Used on a finished stay, This stay is done. Change the dates only if you arrived or left on a different day.
  • For context only. Never for warnings, those are coral lines.

Sheet Bar

The top of every sheet. Leaving always lives here.

  • Three columns. Left a round size-nav-button on line, center the title in title, right an optional text action in purple-text, like Edit.
  • × closes a first screen, like New trip, Settings or a trip overview.
  • ‹ goes back one step inside a flow, like Your visa, the plan, Edit trip, or Design system from Settings.
  • The bottom of a sheet holds only the forward action. Reading pages have no bottom button.
  • Titles never repeat the content below. A saved trip's form is Edit trip, not the country. A stay in a split trip is Stay 2 of 5.

Grouped Rows

Label and value rows in a white card, for forms, rules and details.

  • surface card, 1px line border, radius-card, rows size-row tall with a line divider.
  • Label on the left in body muted, value on the right in body ink.
  • A chevron when the row opens a picker or another step. Read only rows, like Rules for this trip, have no chevron.
  • An empty field shows Choose in muted regular, never a made up value.

Segmented Switch

A two choice switch, like Business or Personal, or Planning and Booked on a trip with one stay.

  • Track in line, thumb in surface with shadow-segment, both radius-pill.
  • Labels in body. The selected one is semibold purple-text, the other muted.
  • In a new trip nothing is selected until the person taps.

Button

The one forward action at the bottom of a sheet or screen, full width.

  • Primary fills primary with white title text, size-button tall, radius-pill.
  • Soft fills soft with purple-text. Only used as the second choice on the empty Today screen, like Add booked trip.
  • One primary per screen. No Cancel or Back buttons at the bottom, leaving lives at the top left.
  • Disabled is the primary at 45 percent opacity.
  • Labels say what happens, like See stays, Save trip or Save.

Switch

An on and off row, used for I booked travel for this stay.

  • A row in a card with the label in body on the left and the switch on the right, 56 tall.
  • Off, the track is chip with a 1.5px chevron outline so it passes 3 to 1. On, the track fills with primary.
  • The whole row is the target, with role switch and its checked state for screen readers.
  • Only shown on stays that have not started. Booked is only what the person says, the app never turns it on.

Principles

  1. One answer per screen. The hero answers one question with one number and one date, like 20 days left and Leave China by Sat, Nov 7. Everything else waits below.
  2. Dark hero, light content. A deep purple hero carries the answer. Content sits below on a warm off white in white cards.
  3. Purple acts, coral warns. Purple is for actions, progress and the stay you are in. Coral only appears when something needs checking.
  4. Never guess for the person. Show only dates the person entered or the app knows. A stay is Booked only when the person says so, never because they saved it.
  5. One way to do each thing. Top left always leaves a sheet, the bottom always moves forward, the same warning always looks the same.

Layout

  • Phones use a 22 side margin on every screen and sheet.
  • Laptops keep the hero colour full width and place content inside max-width of 1040, all on one left edge.
  • Sections start with a title header in ink 22 above the card. A count or note can sit on the right in small. Cards are white with a 1px line border and radius-card. No shadows.
  • The add button floats bottom right and never covers content, the page leaves room under the last card.

Navigation

  • Phones have a bottom tab bar with Today and Trips. Laptops move them to a top bar on hero with Stay Counter on the left.
  • Settings sits behind the gear on Today.
  • A chevron means a row opens something. Rows without one only show information. On laptops only rows with a chevron react to the mouse.
  • Sheets have one rule. Top left leaves, × closes a first screen, ‹ goes back one step. The bottom holds one forward action, like See stays, Save trip or Save. Reading pages, like Settings, have no bottom button.
  • A saved trip opens on its overview first. Edit at the top right opens Edit trip. Viewing never needs the form.

Screens

  • Today. Hero with the answer. Below it the current trip as a timeline, then Upcoming, then Tax days.
  • Trips. Tabs Upcoming and Past. Upcoming shows Now first. Past groups by year. On laptops each trip is one table row with country, dates, days and type.
  • Trip overview. The answer, the stays, Rules for this trip, then Details.
  • New trip. Starts empty. Country, dates, entry and type, then See stays, then Save trip.

Warnings

  • Show each problem once, where it lives.
  • A problem with one stay is a small coral icon next to that stay's dates. The row's screen reader label says the reason, and the stay screen explains it in one sentence.
  • A problem with the whole trip, like a tax limit or a single entry visa, is a count on Trip details, like 1 to check. Without one, Trip details shows the total days.
  • On the trip overview, the rules card says each problem in a small coral-text line under its rule, with one chip under the answer.
  • A new trip's plan, before it is saved, shows its warnings in one coral box, since it has no rules card.

Stays

  • A long trip splits into stays, one per entry. The app suggests where to leave and come back.
  • One line in stay-line joins the stays so they read as one trip. The header beside the trip name counts them, like 6 stays · 4 not booked.
  • Each stay says what it is. Done, Now, Back in N days, Booked or Not booked.
  • Booked is only what the person says. The stay screen has one switch, I booked travel for this stay. It never names flights, people also travel by train, ferry or car.
  • Dots and line show the same thing. Grey dot for done, large dot with a halo for now, filled dot for booked, hollow dot for not booked. The line into a stay is dashed while it is not booked and solid once it is booked, done or now.
  • A trip is Booked when every stay ahead is booked. Otherwise lists say Planning, Not booked or 1 of 2 booked. Only a trip with one stay asks Planning or Booked on Edit trip.
  • Editing a stay moves the stays after it. The edit screen says when the next stay can start.
  • Finished stays can still be corrected. A grey note says so.

Counting

  • Days count both arrival and leave days.
  • Days left count from tomorrow, so the number matches a calendar.
  • Tax days follow each country's own rule, the calendar year for China, the past 12 months elsewhere. Home is not listed.

Writing

  • Plain words, sentence case, short. No colons, semicolons or dashes in UI text.
  • Date ranges are the one exception and use a spaced en dash, like Nov 1 – 30. A range into the next year names the year once, like Nov 1 – Mar 31, 2027.
  • Weekdays only on single dates people act on, like Sat, Nov 7.
  • Calm words for problems, like rules to check, never broken.
  • Keep Not legal advice on any screen that shows rules.
